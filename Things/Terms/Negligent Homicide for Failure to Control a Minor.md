## General
---

Violation of Penal Code sec. 41-4

Punishable by up to 10 years in prison and a fine of up to $100,000

## To Convict
---

> Under the relevant Texoma Penal Code statute, to be convicted of the crime of Negligent Homicide for Failure to Control a Minor, the Prosecution must prove the following elements beyond a reasonable doubt: 
> 
> 	1. The Defendant had a legal duty to exercise reasonable control over a minor child to prevent the child from intentionally harming others or acting in a way that creates a serious risk of bodily harm to others; 
> 
> 	2. From facts known to the Defendant, the Defendant reasonably should have known of the necessity of exercising sufficient control to prevent the minor child from harming others or acting in a way that creates a serious risk of bodily harm to others; 
> 
> 	3. The Defendant failed to exercise such control; 
> 
> 	4. The Defendant’s failure was a direct cause of death to Julien Younger.
> 
> The Defendant’s failure does not need to be the sole or immediate cause of Younger’s death, and there may be more than one cause responsible for the death of a person. It is enough that Defendant’s failure to act as required by law was a substantial factor in the chain of events that led to Younger’s death. 
> 
> The first element is undisputed. Only the second, third, and fourth elements are at issue at trial.


>The following is a true and accurate excerpt from Texoma Penal Code: TPC Sec. 41 – Homicide
>
	**Sec. 41-4: Negligent Homicide for Failure to Control a Minor**
>	A. A person is guilty of Negligent Homicide for Failure to Control a Minor if they 
>		a. Have legal custody of a minor child; 
>		b. Based on facts known to them, they reasonably should have known of the necessity of exercising sufficient control over the minor to prevent the child from intentionally harming others or acting in a way that creates a serious risk of bodily harm or death to others; and 
>		c. That failure is a direct cause of the death of another person. 
>	B. Negligent Homicide for Failure to Control a Minor is punishable by up to ten (10) years imprisonment and a fine of up to $100,000.
